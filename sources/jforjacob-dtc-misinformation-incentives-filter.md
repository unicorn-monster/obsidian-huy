The majority of the mis-information in the DTC space is because of misaligned incentives from agency owners, info sellers & the brands they service

Remember it is an agencies objective to make as much money as possible from brands with the least amount of work possible

It is an info sellers objective to convince you that their specific strategies are right and everything you are currently doing or that is mainstream is wrong

When you start running everything you see on DTC Twitter (and who it's coming from) through this filter everything makes a lot more sense

An agency isn't going to promote something that's going to require more manual work on their part. More hours for them, less margin

An info seller isn't going to concede their position when a new and better way of doing things comes around. They're already invested too deep into convincing their way is the best and things would crumble if they had to pivot

I honestly believe for the most part brand owners should just listen to other brand owners. Very rarely you see brand owners arguing over creative testing or cost caps with each other etc. They don't really give a shit, they just wanna do whatever works. On the other hand you see agencies ready to die to defend their philosophies

It's no wonder so many brand owners are confused. The majority of the info they are being fed doesn't serve them

There are exceptions of course, there are some agencies & info sellers out there really just trying to be successful by making their clients as much money as possible, but it's honestly rare
