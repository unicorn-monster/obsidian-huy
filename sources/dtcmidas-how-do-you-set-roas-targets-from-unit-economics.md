That’s not the right way to think about it.

ROAS doesn’t tell you if you’re profitable. Your unit economics do.

You should be asking: “What’s my target CAC/ROAS based on my unit economics?”

Here’s what you should look at (I advice to track and check this on a daily basis):

- AOV

- Landed cost: cogs + fulfillment

- Contribution margin 2 (cm2): aov - landed cost (how much is left to acquire a customer)

- Your target CAC:  Based on your cm2, LTV and cash flow, how much can you afford to spend to acquire a customer profitably without running out of cash. 

When starting out, just set your target CAC below your CM2.

That way you’re profitable on first order. 

Later when you have a better view on your unit economics, you can increase your target CAC to make scaling easier.

Once you know your target CAC, you work backwards to figure out what ROAS you need.

Example:

- AOV: $100
- Landed cost: $25
- CM2: $75
- Desired profit margin: $25
- Target CAC: $50 ($75 CM2 - $25 desired profit)

You need 2+ ROAS to hit that.

Yes, this is very simplified and doesn’t account for a lot of things.

But it shows you how to think about setting these metrics based on your actual unit economics, not arbitrary ROAS targets.
