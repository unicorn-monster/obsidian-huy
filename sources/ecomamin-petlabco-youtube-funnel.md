pet lab co's best youtube ad has 110.9M views and NOT ONE of their live youtube ads sends people to a product page

a 56-second yeast ad running since june 2025, a "warning sign" advertorial for almost every symptom a dog owner googles, and a subscription offer that's already picked for you before you scroll

the youtube ads

avatar and ICP: a dog owner watching their dog do something weird and not knowing why. paw licking, itchy ears, eating grass, paws that smell like corn chips. nobody in these ads is shopping for a supplement. they're worried about their dog

word choice: "this is a sign of yeast growth" said 3 times back to back before anything else happens. "if your dog eats grass, do this every day." "stop what you're doing right now." every opener is something the owner has already seen at home

hook and pacing: all 13 videos they've run in the last 90 days open on a symptom. not one opens on the product. 12 of the 13 are vertical and 60 seconds or under. the chew only shows up once the owner is convinced there's a problem

no vet on camera either. the authority comes from the claims ("clinically studied", "2.3 million owners") and the vets show up later, on the page

the launch pattern: they don't retire winners, they remake them. the yeast script keeps getting re-cut with new headlines (the latest one went live 14 sep). their grass-eating video did 21.6M views, got rebuilt with AI, and the AI version has been live since april. the internal video titles carry hook versions like "H1", "H2" and "HOOK 02", so they test new openings on the same body

the part that surprised me: 61 of their video ads were live this week last year. right now it's 5, all selling one product. they launched 90 new videos in october 2025 and 2 so far this october. that's my read off the transparency center dates, google doesn't say why an ad stops

now onto where all that traffic actually lands

4 of their 5 live youtube ads go to the same page, a "6 reasons vets love..." advertorial. vet in the hero shot, an "experts' choice" badge, "5.7 million owners" under the button. the #1 ad goes to a stat-heavy sales page with two named US vets on it

search is where it gets clever. every symptom gets its own ad and its own page:

"is your dog eating grass? - is it a warning sign?"
"dog won't stop paw licking? - try this #1 selling probiotic"
"is your dog scooting? - try anal gland supplements"

problem searches go to "is it a warning sign?" advertorials, product and offer searches go to offer pages, and only their brand name goes to the homepage

branded search is locked down too. promo extension for 40% off, a 4.0 star seller rating from 11,419 reviews, "3 prices from $0.75/day", and separate "reviews" and "login" ads. they're covering every way someone types their name

6 new products got search ads in the last 4 weeks, and they've started running spanish-language ads

shopping titles lead with the problem, not the brand. the same dental powder shows up as "teeth cleaning dog dental...", "plaque-reducing dog dental..." and "probright advanced dog...". that's title testing in the feed

then the offer: subscribe is pre-selected. 1 tub $28.08, 2 for $44.93, 3 for $56.16, with the price per chew shown ($0.94, $0.75, $0.62), a free gift on the first order and six 50%-off add-ons in the buy box

the through line in all this is that every click lands on a page built for where the owner's head is at

- the youtube ad sells the symptom before the chew is ever on screen
- the search ad meets the owner mid-worry with "is it a warning sign?" and a page that answers it
- the offer page closes with vets, proof and a subscription that's already ticked

nobody gets asked to buy until they believe something's wrong with their dog, and it's why pet lab co can keep one 56-second video running for over a year
