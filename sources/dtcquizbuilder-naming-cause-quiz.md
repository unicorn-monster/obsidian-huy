This funnel copy is built around naming the cause before showing any product

On cold traffic, pitching a product first just sends people to Amazon to price-shop

So the page installs a diagnosis before the buyer ever meets a price

Moerie does this with their hair quiz

The ad hooks thinning hair, and the first taps collect symptoms

Then before any bottle shows up, you hit a dead-stop slide:

"Have you heard of scalp age?"

Just a decline chart & a "Got it" button {you have to click it to move on}

Now thinning hair isn't just bad luck, it has a name

Leafly runs the same play on their nervous system quiz

The section labels update like a clinic intake form: profile, physical signals, impact

Before any plan is pitched, the page installs "vagal tone"

It isn't "buy our routine"

It's "your vagal tone is low, and that's why nothing worked"

Amua sets it up with a failed-solutions inventory

A multi-select asks: "What have you tried?"

Gym, diets, supplements

Then the immediate follow-up: "Did any of it work?"

When the buyer taps no, the copy absolves them: it wasn't your fault, it was the wrong mechanism

A generic symptom gets compared against cheap alternatives

A named cause demands a specific fix

By the offer, comparing prices feels pointless. The buyer already agreed to the diagnosis
