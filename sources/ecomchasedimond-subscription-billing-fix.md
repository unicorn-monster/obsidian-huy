title: Fix subscription churn billing cadence

A supplements brand paid me to fix their winback flow. I rewrote it three times over two months. Churn didn't move a point.

Then I looked at their billing.

They charged monthly. So every 30 days a customer got a shipping notice and a charge on their card for a bottle they were two thirds of the way through.

Twelve times a year this brand walked up to its best customers and asked whether they still wanted this.

I had spent two months rewriting the email that shows up at the twelfth one.

What we changed: a three month term at a real per-unit discount, offered on the order confirmation page instead of the product page.

Before the sale, asking for a longer commitment can cost you the order. After the card clears, it can't.

47% of new subscribers took the three month option in the first 30 days. That moves a subscriber from twelve cancel decisions a year down to four.

Their cash moved forward too. Three months of revenue now lands on day one, which is what let them raise daily spend in 3 months without financing it.

Projects to roughly $20k collected in month one

Modeled from subscriber count, current monthly churn and the take rate above. Not measured yet, it shipped last week.

The rest of what surfaced once I stopped looking at emails:

- A 30 day refill cadence on a product that lasts 45 days. Every renewal arrived before anyone needed it
- Winback discount running at 25%, roughly double what this cohort needed to come back
- Signup offered no cadence choice, so everyone defaulted to the option that churns fastest
- Renewal notice going out 3 days ahead. That is three days of thinking about it

Curious: how many times a year do you make your subscribers decide?
