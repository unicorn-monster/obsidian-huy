Your offer is going to be the most important lever for BFCM.

Here's how 5 of the biggest brands structured theirs last year and what to take away from each:

1. AG1 ran one offer and changed the framing, not the number

They put 26% off the first subscription order out on Nov 27 and held it all the way through Dec 4. The deal itself never changed — they just found a new angle for it on each send. It started as an announcement, turned into a reminder, became a last call, and then once Black Friday was over they pivoted it into a subscription savings push.

Lesson: when you are running one offer, give it different labels through different time periods to keep newness. You get the same send volume without giving away more margin.

2. IM8 focused on their Quarterly offer

They gave away four bonus products worth $118 with the quarterly subscription and never ran a sitewide percentage off at all.

Lesson: if you want customers to buy something… just focus your BFCM on that. Stack the bonuses and value around that key entry point.

3. Create Wellness front loaded bundles and saved the sitewide for later

They opened with early access to bundles plus a free mystery gift on anything over $100, then went up to 53% off sitewide from Nov 27 through Dec 3. Right before Christmas they came back in with a free travel supply on subscription orders.

Lesson: run your bundles in the early access window and your sitewide after. You pull AOV up before the discount wars start, then hit everyone once they're actually shopping.

4. Gymshark tiered their offer by time period

They ran an announcement window with markdowns up to 50%, then opened early access at up to 50% off everything, and only during Black Friday weekend did they stack an extra 25% on top of it.

Lesson: hold your strongest version for the weekend itself. You get three weeks of sale and nobody who buys early feels burned when the real deal lands.

5. Cuts Clothing never changed their offer at all

They ran up to 60% off from Oct 31 all the way through Dec 25 without ever touching the number. What kept it alive was weekly product and color drops rather than a deeper discount.

Lesson: if you've got product dropping, let the drops carry the urgency instead of the discount. One offer can run two months that way.

Summary: every offer works… it's just a matter of what makes the most sense for your brand.
