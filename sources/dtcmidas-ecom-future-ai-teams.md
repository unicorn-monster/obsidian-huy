title: Ecom future with AI: smaller teams and global competition
---

I think it's the best time ever to be in ecom.

Physical products are way harder to replace with AI than digital products. AI can help you build an app/saas/write books/make music/movies, but people still need tangible products manufactured and delivered to them.

That's part of what makes this industry durable, but how we operate in it will change a lot.

1. Acquisition will get way more personalized.

Very specific ads and funnels for very specific people and problems, at scale.

Before, building all of those took a lot of time and people, with ai, you can go much more specific without needing a huge team.

2. Teams will get much smaller.

One good person with AI can cover what used to be several roles. I think we'll see more roles like:

- Growth engineer: performance marketing, media buying, CRO, A/B testing, unit economics and analytics.
- Creative engineer: creative strategist, editor and designer in one.
- Operations engineer: supply chain, payments, customer service, sourcing and procurement.
- Product engineer: customer research, product development, R&D, testing, quality and continuous improvement.

Not saying every company will have exactly these four roles, but that's the direction I see things going.

3. Competition will get more global.

As supply chains get more global and localization gets easier, bigger brands will be able to enter smaller markets with fully localized ads and funnels.

I also think Chinese manufacturers will get much better at branding and marketing.

They already know how to make the products. As they get better at selling them too, I think they'll take a bigger share of some categories, like we're seeing with cars.

So what stays defensible?

- Actual creativity, combined with acquisition and distribution. Being able to spot something, jump on a trend and execute better than the rest.
- An actual good product. Doing R&D and continuously making what you sell better.
- And brand. Making people genuinely care about your company and product. But that's much harder to build.

If I were starting now, I'd prioritize learning acquisition and distribution.

Ecom is already extremely competitive, learning how to acquire customers in this industry gives you skills you can take into plenty of other industries too.

So yes, I'm very bullish on ecom.

I wouldn't assume the way we do it today will work forever, but I think it's a great industry to build in and a great place to build transferable skills.
