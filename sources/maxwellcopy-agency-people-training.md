Unfortunately this is the case with most agencies.

The main PRO of working with an agency is getting proven systems, strategic insight from managing a lot of different accounts, and team members with real experience in that specific niche or service.

The CON is that they often just won't care as much as a day 1 brand employee does, or have as much focus as in-house team member would.

This is natural, as your agency team will have other clients and just won't care the same way.

But when you sign with an agency you're hoping the proven systems and strategic insight parts outweigh the focus parts...

The truth is, any agency WILL drop the ball sometimes.

I consider us a great agency. In my biased opinion, I'd say we're among the best...

But almost every single month we have brands leave us, sometimes not our fault, but every now and then it is.

Which is why if you're running an agency you should be generous with refunds and free months of service.

You won't crush it every single time, so it's your job to make it right, and you should have budget allocated every month to deal with these situations.

In my opinion there are 3 parts that go into building a great agency:

1) The people you hire. 90% of everything is whether you have smart, competent, talented individuals.

2) Trainings. Daily trainings, and forcing your team to actually act on the insights the leaders put together so they're always evolving.

3) Your systems. Base SOPs, your communication cadence, reporting templates, all of it.

Most agencies have point number 3 down.

They've got maybe 50% of point number 1 (so about half their team is actually good).

And they're very minimal on point number 2.

Where as in reality agency owners should be focused on 1 and 2 rather than pouring time into 3.

It's the least important and it's what most people spend the most time on.
