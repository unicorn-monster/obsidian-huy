Everyone analyzes their winning ads to figure out what works.

Almost no one analyzes their losing ads to figure out what kills performance.

This is survivorship bias.

Te same mistake that almost killed Allied pilots in WWII.

During WWII, the military studied planes returning from combat missions.

They mapped out where the bullet holes were - mostly in the fuselage and wings.

So they made a plan: reinforce those areas with armor.

Statistician Wald said they had it backwards.

The returning planes showed where a plane CAN take damage and still survive.

The bullet holes in the fuselage and wings weren't the problem.

The problem was the areas with NO damage - the engines and cockpit.

Why?

Because planes hit in those spots never made it back. 

They crashed.

You're not seeing them in your data.

Wald recommended armoring the parts with no bullet holes.

Those were the fatal zones.

This is survivorship bias:

You only analyze what survived and ignore everything that didn't make it.

Same thing happens with your ads.

You look at winners and think "this hook worked, this angle worked and this format worked."

But you're only seeing the survivors.

You're ignoring  the ads that died or never even got any spend.

Those losing ads tell you what DOESN'T work.

That's equally valuable data.

Most people run 50 ads, find 3 winners, and only study those 3.

They're missing the lessons from the 47 that died in testing.

Go through your losing ads and try to understand why they didn’t work.

- Weak hooks?
- Wrong awareness stage?
- Unclear value prop?
- Format doesn’t fit the audience?
- Bad creative execution?

Knowing what NOT to do eliminates bad bets before you waste budget on them.
