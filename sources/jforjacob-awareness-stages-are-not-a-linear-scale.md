title: "Don’t assume audiences get bigger as you move up the awareness stages"
---

A big mistake I see people making is assuming that all the stages of awareness apply to their product and that the size of the audience they can reach is bigger as they go up awareness stages

e.g that there are more people in the 'unaware' stage than there is the 'product aware' stage. This is not true and you could be spending needless amounts of time and energy marketing to these segments

A good example I always like to give is with backpacks. The size of the segment of people in the unaware stage is much, much lower than those that are product aware

You would be wasting your time trying to market to people who are unaware here or even problem aware, thinking that you are going to unlock a bigger audience to sell to. In reality almost all of your effort should go into product aware marketing here (showcasing differences in aesthetics, features, materials, pricing etc)

Basically the point of this post is to say don't automatically assume that you'll unlock bigger and bigger audiences as you move from fully aware up to unaware. You need to analyse the market sophistication level first

For some products the biggest pool of people will be in the product aware stage, for others it will be problem aware, for others it will be unaware. It's not a linear scale
