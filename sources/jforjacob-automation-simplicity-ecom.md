title: Complexity is the enemy in ecom automation

Before e-commerce I was an engineer specialising in automation systems

I would design and install automation systems in hospitals for surgery theatres, pharmaceutical labs and storage facilities, large government office buildings etc

Controlling everything like the temperature systems, humidity, ventilation, pressure, vital equipment, alarm systems, emergency shutdowns

And something I learned during that time is that the more people wanted sophisticated systems with complex automations, the client satisfaction and usability of the system declined

It looked great at handover but as time went on the clients would forget how certain things worked, a fault on one piece of equipment would start a cascade that ended up in large parts of their building being un-usable with no way of manually over-riding things, they'd need to employ people full time to look after the system of have us called out frequently for maintenance

This time of my life led me to be someone that much that much prefers things when they are stupidly simple

And it was the same with a lot of our engineers. We would be building these ridiculously complex system for clients but in our own homes, we wouldn't even have a thermostat for our heating. Just a simple on or off button

This obviously has spilled over to e-commerce

I've seen lots of guides on how to automate parts of the business and when I look into it I immediate just know it's gonna be a nightmare to actual use and maintain

I think back to open claw and how much time people were spending there to setup what in my mind could have been a lot simpler. Plus there was always bugs and you need to keep reconnecting things etc

It's only recently that I think the tech is there with ecommerce to make simple automation systems and I've started fully embracing it

Like with our customer service. The only reason we are at at automation rate of over 90% is because it's super simple to do it now, and the system itself has few decencies, points of failure or maintenance (shout Commslayer)

Same with bots and automations there

I hated openclaw, it just filled me with frustration

But the bots now like Grok Bot are super simple and make automating parts of the business super simple and maintenance free

But even with these bots, don't think you need to do anything crazy to get good output

There seems to be a tendency for people to think that the more steps, the more context and the more information they give these things the better. This is not true

Always start with the absolute bare necessities of what you need for the system to work. Run it for a while and see if the output is good or if you do actually need to add another step or add more context somewhere etc

Do not just blindly put parts in without first testing if the system can work without that part

I think if I showed people exactly how we've automated our customer service or our static ads etc they would be incredibly underwhelmed

And that is the point you want to reach with any automated system, that it is underwhelmingly simple. Like a 5 year old could have came up with it

Complexity is the enemy in automation

Start with the end goal and work backwards only adding things that are absolutely needed to achieve that

No "ah that would be cool to add" or "that's super clever let's do that"

If you really want to ramp things up you can add negative feedback loops into the equation (which basically means the system will self improve and correct itself)

For any nerds go do some research on PID loops

This is only added after the system functions though
