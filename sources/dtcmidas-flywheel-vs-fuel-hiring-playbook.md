The difference between a 7-figure and 8-figure ecom brand is your ability to attract talent and build a great team.

Here's my exact playbook for recruiting A-players.

I group hires into two categories: flywheel and fuel hires.

Flywheels are great at strategy AND execution.

They come up with ideas and ship them end-to-end. 

They build systems and processes that keep generating value long-term.

Important: I'd never hire someone who can only "strategize" but can't execute. Flywheels do BOTH.

Examples: growth leads, creative strategists, operations managers, retention marketers.

Fuel hires are great executors.

They add fuel into existing systems, but the output stops when they stop working.

More linear input/output.

Examples: Customer service agents, video editors, designers, store managers.

How to sequence your hires?

In the beginning, hire fuel first. You need to be the flywheel.

First hires should be editors, designers, customer service agents, and a store manager.

Once you're running smoothly, add your first flywheel hire - like a creative strategist or growth lead.

After this flywheel is onboarded, hire more fuel to support them.

More editors means more output from your creative strategist.

The more flywheels you have, the more fuel you can add, the more your output increases.

Where to find them?

For flywheels, I use linkedin recruiter. 

Post your job, select your regions, add $10-30/day to boost reach (more budget won't work better)

Also you can poach talent.

Hit up people in similar roles at other brands and agencies.

For fuel, I've had success with linkedIn, here on X, upwork, and niche facebook groups (editors and designers).

When you have people applying and showing interest for the role DON’T start vetting them immediately.

First make them VERY excited about the role and your company, then vet them.

Show them how fast you're growing, your vision, big numbers (revenue, growth rate, customers), good reviews, influencers you're working with.

Get them wanting the job before you evaluate if they're right for it.

Also only hire when you're very sure they're good and fit the team & culture. 

Don't hire out of desperation. It will fail 19/20 times.

This is the process I follow:

For flywheels: intro interview → case study + presentation → 2-week paid trial → hire

For fuel: portfolio/experience check → small practical case (like "here's the product page, make a 3 static ad concepts") → 1 interview → hire

Fire quickly if they don’t perform. 

One B-player can turn your A-players into B-players.

Flywheels should show impact in the first month.

If they're not delivering strategic value by then, they probably won't.

Fuel should consistently hit quality and speed benchmarks.

If they're not, cut ties fast.
