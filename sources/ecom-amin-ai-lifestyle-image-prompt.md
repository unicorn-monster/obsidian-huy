the number one reason AI-generated google ads creatives look like GARBAGE is because the prompts describe products instead of scenes

here's the principle behind EVERY AI creative that converts and the exact prompting framework i use

a product on a white background communicates "commodity." a product in a real-world environment communicates "identity." for google shopping listings, lifestyle images outperform white background shots by 30-50% on CTR across MOST ecom categories

the shopping grid is a visual marketplace where EVERY brand competes for the same click. white background shots look identical. lifestyle shots stop the eye

but MOST people prompting AI image generators write something like "generate an image of a leather wallet on a white background, high quality, 4K"

that's how you generate stock slop. the generic, over-processed, fake-looking imagery that screams "this was the cheapest option"

here's what the prompt should describe instead:

the environment: a modern coffee shop table with natural daylight coming through a window to the left. warm, golden hour quality. soft shadows

the human element: a man in his early 30s, sleeve slightly rolled up, mid-conversation with someone out of frame. the product is part of who he is, not something he's posing with

the imperfection details: a coffee ring stain on the table. a notebook open to a half-written page. an espresso half-finished. these details prevent the image from looking AI-generated because perfection is what triggers the "this is fake" response in the viewer

the lighting direction: warm light for lifestyle products. cool light for tech and performance products. dramatic contrast for luxury and premium. the lighting tells the viewer how to feel about the product BEFORE they read a word of copy

the camera: medium close-up. shallow depth of field. background slightly blurred. the composition should look like someone captured a candid moment on a high-end phone, not like a studio shoot

when you prompt with scene-level specificity instead of product-level simplicity, the output from models like Soul or Seedream through Higgsfield MCP produces imagery that passes as professional lifestyle photography
