Knix ran a TV ad during The Voice, and it outperformed every "obvious" placement they bought.

9,000 site visits in five minutes. CPA 70% lower than their benchmark.

Here's the backstory: Knix was already doing TV, but they decided to stop assuming they knew where their audience was. Instead of sticking to the networks you'd expect for a women's intimacy brand, they tested across news, crime, and general entertainment.

The unexpected placements won. One airing on The Voice drove that 9,000-visit spike. Crime and general entertainment outperformed their traditional buys.

Then they kept testing creative on the placements that were working. Response rate doubled. Cost per view dropped 45%.

Most ecommerce brands treat TV like a giant brand bet. One big buy, hope it works, move on. Knix treated it like a paid media channel. Test placements, measure response, cut what doesn't work, scale what does.

That's the playbook more DTC brands should be running.
