we doubled a brand's google shopping conversion rate by changing three words in their product titles

no new campaign, no changed bid strategy, no budget increase. three words in the product feed rearranged. let me show you what happened

google shopping indexes the first three words of your product title harder than anything else, and mobile cuts the title after word four

9 out of 10 accounts i audit have titles structured like this:

"BrandName Premium Collagen Peptides 500mg"

the buyer typed "collagen supplement for joints"

google reads "BrandName Premium Collagen" and has zero context to match the listing to the query

the fix:

[primary keyword] + [benefit angle] + [brand name last]

"Collagen Supplement for Joint Pain + Skin | BrandName"

we ran the split test on keyword-first vs brand-first with the same products, bids, and budget

conversion rate doubled, ROAS nearly doubled, from rearranging words already sitting in the title

and the cascade goes deeper than the immediate CVR lift

when shopping listings convert at 2x the rate, google's algorithm rewards you with better placements. better placements bring more impressions at lower CPCs. lower CPCs bring better ROAS at the same spend

one title change cascades through the entire account

and your meta ads are making this worse. your meta ad generates category interest. the buyer opens google, types "best collagen supplement," and your listing doesn't show because your title starts with your brand name. your competitor's listing shows because their title starts with "collagen supplement"

you paid the CPM on meta and they captured the conversion on google. the only barrier between you and the sale was the first three words of a product title

70-80% of ecom ad revenue on google runs through shopping. shopping runs off your feed. if the titles are wrong, every campaign in the account is fighting with one hand behind its back

go into your merchant center, pull your top 10 products by revenue, and look at the first three words

if any start with your brand name, you're losing money today
