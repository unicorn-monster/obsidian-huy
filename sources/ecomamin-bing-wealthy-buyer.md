41% of bing users in the US earn over $100,000 a year

almost nobody in ecom advertises to them

and once you understand WHO is sitting on that platform, ignoring it starts to look insane

the median bing user is 45-65. desktop-heavy. professional or retired. searching from a work laptop that defaults to Edge, with an outlook inbox that opens to MSN

they never switched search engines because they don't care which one is cooler

what makes them valuable isn't only the income though

it's that they've been exposed to a FRACTION of the blackhat ecom advertising everyone else has been burned by

they haven't been retargeted by fourteen brands selling the same aliexpress product with different logos. they haven't hit a fake countdown timer this month

so the scepticism reflex that fires in 2 seconds on instagram fires much softer here

now the economics

CPCs run 20-40% lower than the identical keywords on google, because 90% of ecom brands have never opened a microsoft ads account

cheaper clicks, wealthier buyer, higher trust, lower competition. that combination doesn't exist anywhere else in paid search right now

we launched a fresh bing account for a client in health. zero history, no brand recognition on the platform

four weeks later: $20,000 revenue from $2,800 spend. 7.1x

setup is one afternoon. microsoft ads has a one-click import that copies your entire google account across. campaigns, ad groups, keywords, ad copy, extensions, all of it

drop bids 20-30% after importing because google bid levels overbid on bing

two things that get accounts banned, since this is where people quit

landing page quality gets manually reviewed harder than google. working contact page, visible privacy policy, clear refund terms, no aggressive pop-ups on first visit

and editorial rules are stricter. ALL CAPS headlines and unsubstantiated superlatives get disapproved. write clean copy, save personality for the landing page

start branded search at $20-30/day for 7-14 days to build account trust before scaling

categories that print hardest: supplements, home goods, premium accessories, pet, fitness equipment
