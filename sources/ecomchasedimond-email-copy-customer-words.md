title: Email copy starts with customer words — research framework
---

When I write email copy, I like to start with the words customers are already using.

I spend a lot of time combing through reviews, support tickets, post-purchase surveys, and even call transcripts.

Their words aren't the finished copy, as they're usually specific and raw. But this is what makes them incredibly useful.

They show you:

- What was happening when the customer started looking
- What almost stopped them from buying
- What finally gave them the confidence to buy
- How they describe the result in their own words

These are details that are hard to come up with when you're staring at a blank page.

Before your next campaign, spend 20 minutes doing this:

1. Pull 15-20 recent reviews, support tickets, survey responses, or call notes.
2. Highlight anything about the trigger, hesitation, or result.
3. Group similar phrases together and look for what keeps coming up.
4. Pick one repeated idea and use it to shape the subject line, opening, proof, and CTA.

I'm not copying and pasting customer quotes into every email. But I'm looking for patterns that help me understand what matters to the customer and how they naturally talk about it.

It almost always gives me a better place to start than a blank page.
