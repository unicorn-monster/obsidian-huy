Just finished up an interesting round of testing

We kept everything identical with these statics even the angle but just changed how the message was positioned

one headline was positively framed while the other was more negatively framed and gave a command

Positive framing example - "Get results like {alternative solution}, without the {negative side effect}"

Negative framing - "Ditch the {alternative solution}, get the {solution} without the {negative side effect}"

The positively framed headline performed shockingly better. 23x spend vs the negatively framed headline and CPA of £13 vs £78

These are results across 5 separate individual tests. In each one of those tests the positively framed headline was the run away winner

This is a key learning you can only get by isolating single variables in your testing. I'm not a fan of the recent advice going around to make every variation distinctly different. Impossible to know what is driving the difference in performance in those scenarios

Variety is a key thing to have in your ad account but in my opinion that should be done on a batch by batch basis not on individual ad variations

If I had of changed the layout of the static, the messaging, the framing of the message, the colours etc I would have no meaningful learning to take away other than "this one got more spend" ok, but why? No meaningful take away to be had like that
