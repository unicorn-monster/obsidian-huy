title: "ASC only looks like retargeting when your manual sales campaigns starve it of reach"

One of the most misunderstood things in media buying I see today is ASC

I see so many people say it doesn't work for them, 'it only targets bottom of funnel' or 'it's just re-targetting'

Something I know from my own experience to be absolutely false

Almost all the people I know who say this have one thing in common - They're allocating the majority of their budget to other campaign types

We know that ASC is very good at finding high intent shoppers. We know that the number of people we can reach with our ads is relatively limited. So what happens when you're allocating significant budget to manual sales campaigns while also running ASC?

The manual sales campaigns (usually at much higher budgets) are going to have a lot more reach than your ASC and very much limit the number of people it can effectively target. Because the manual sales campaigns are at higher budgets they are the campaigns reaching most of the 'top of funnel' audience, leaving the ASC with not much else other than people who have already been hit with ads from your other campaigns. Which is why people think they just 're-target'

But what happens if you turn off those manual sales campaigns? Who is the ASC going to 're-target'? You see what I'm getting at?

In my experience ASC works best as an 'all of funnel' campaign. When you give it a wide variety of ads tailored to different awareness stages and formats, and it's free to target the entire pool of your audience, that's when it seems to work its magic

Even with running all my ASC's with cost controls, I consistently see ads create their own 'funnel' with ads driving awareness and other ads sweeping up the sales

'Top of funnel' ads are usually getting the highest spend, lowest frequency and worst CPA's but these actually allow you to spend a lot more overall in the campaign. They are 'feeding' the other ads

'bottom of funnel' style ads are getting lower spend, higher frequency and low CPA

With ASC & cost controls, your target is set at the campaign level, so it gives Meta the room to spend more at a worse CPA than those ads that are great at driving awareness and getting people interested in your brand, and it will balance it out with the ads that are great at getting the sale but aren't capable of large spend. Overall the target CPA will level out to your target

This is also why I don't recommend running ASC, especially with cost controls to people until they have a selection of ads that can work together like this and create an 'all of funnel' type situation. That really does seem to be what ASC is great at

I decided to go all in on ASC for my main campaigns around 8 months ago and can honestly say I haven't had any of the issues I see people complaining about. I have no issues which driving awareness, results and CPA are stable and my ads aren't 'fatiguing'

So my advice is, if you have a library with a wide range of varied creatives, try throwing them all in an ASC, turning off your 'BAU' campaigns and see what happens
