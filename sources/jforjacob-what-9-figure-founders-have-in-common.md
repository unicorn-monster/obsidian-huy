I've got a couple of 9 figure friends I talk to almost daily. Both bootstrapped, both did it in an incredibly short amount of time. Both savages

Some things I've noticed they share in common:

They are sponges for information. Always asking questions. Even though they could easily have the mindset that they've got this shit figured out, they don't act like that in any way. So easy to disregard other people's opinions when you're clearly crushing it and doing better than almost everyone

They are incredibly open minded. I'm often baffled that they look for my advice on stuff or want to know how I'm doing certain things. I'm way below their level but yet they still stay curious. Likely just taking the meat and leaving the bones anywhere they can. But it's humbled me a lot to see how these guys still have the student mindset. Never once have I got the impression from them that they feel they are some all-knowing oracle. Which I have seen from people at much, much lower levels

They move ridiculously fast. The stuff I used to think would take months and years to do these guys are getting done in days and weeks. Stuff that I would take forever just thinking about they just get stuck in straight away and just figure out as they go. Good example: a couple weeks ago in the time it took me to hire a new graphic designer my friend had planned and started work on a warehouse expansion for one of his brands based on growing demand he'd seen the previous weeks

They experiment heavily. Constantly testing new things, keeping their ears to the ground, trying to find an edge somewhere and willing to loose money in that pursuit, knowing that the alternative is likely stagnation and inevitable death of the business

They have a clear long term plan. It's one of the things I've noticed the most. The clarity they have in the things they are doing and why. In my short time in ecommerce I've mostly found myself being reactive. Just doing things on a whim and blowing in the wind a bit. I think it helps them a lot when things aren't going their way, they can zoom out and course correct easier when they know exactly what path they are trying to go down. I suppose it's easier to get back on track when you have a clear idea of what the track actually looks like. I think it also helps plan their next moves when they have this kind of clarity. Again, I've found myself stuck a lot of times asking myself "well, what do I do now?" I end up just doing the same stuff over and over instead of actually pushing forward into the next thing

In summary: Always be the student. Stay humble. Move fast. Always be testing. Have a plan and stick to it
