lumin does around $1M/mo selling men's skincare in one of the most saturated niches on the internet

they're running 40+ active creatives on google ads right now

and the whole model rests on one structural decision most ecom brands are too scared to make

their front end offer is a free trial

free product, you pay shipping, 21 days to decide. everyone else in the category leads with a 10% code or a bundle discount

look at what happens in the shopping grid

every competitor listing shows a price of $34 or $49 or $28, so the buyer scans the row and every option asks for money before proving anything

lumin's listing asks for shipping

their click cost sits in the same range as everyone else while their click rate sits well above it. friction at the entry point is the single biggest lever on cold traffic and they stripped the entry friction out

the free trial earns them the customer. the money arrives later

the structure behind the trial:

day 21 hits and the trial auto-converts into a subscription. new products ship on a recurring schedule, and the buyer sets their own frequency, modifies, or cancels whenever

so a customer acquired for the price of shipping now carries a 6, 12, 18 month LTV

the first purchase stops mattering to the model. they're playing a different game to every brand trying to profit on order one

two more pieces worth stealing

founder-led branding. a real person attached to the brand, on camera, explaining why the product exists. the scepticism reflex is trained to distrust logos, not faces

and a VSL doing the heavy lifting on the landing page instead of a spec sheet PDP

so the full stack reads: low-friction acquisition, subscription conversion, high LTV, with founder trust and video carrying the persuasion

the offer does the strategic work. the ads handle delivery

most brands try to fix cold traffic with better creative while their front end asks $49 from someone who met them 8 seconds ago
