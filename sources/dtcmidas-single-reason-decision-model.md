This mental model changed how I make every decision.

I got it from Peter Thiel.

Most people make terrible decisions because they try to optimize for everything at once.

Here’s the better approach:

Divide your reasoning into separate parts and identify the most important factor.

If there’s no single reason strong enough to justify your decision on its own, don’t do it.

What most people do wrong:

They make pros and cons lists, assuming every factor has equal weight, which is lazy and flawed thinking.

They get distracted by “extra benefits” that don’t matter for their actual goal:

- “It has this feature too” when evaluating SaaS tools.

- “This will also boost brand awareness” when you’re trying to increase conversions.

- “This influencer has 2M followers” when you need high-converting traffic, not reach.

What you should do:

Ask yourself: “What’s the ONE reason this decision makes sense?”

If you can’t give a single compelling reason that justifies it completely, step back.

You’re probably not thinking hard enough about what actually matters.

Pick the option that serves your primary purpose best.

Everything else is just noise that won’t give you the results you want.

Most “strategic” decisions fail because people try to solve 5 problems with 1 solution.

Solve one problem really well instead.
