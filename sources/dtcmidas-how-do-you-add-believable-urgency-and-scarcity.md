If you want your offers to convert better, you should add believable urgency and scarcity.

Before I give you examples of how to apply believable urgency and scarcity, you need to understand how it works

People fear losing something more than they value gaining it. Loss aversion is hardwired into the consumer's mind.

When you add believable urgency or scarcity, you're triggering fomo.

It shifts their thinking from "I'll buy this later" to "I need to buy this now before I miss out."

The key word here is BELIEVABLE.

Consumers are not stupid.

Countdown timers that reset on every visit, stock warnings that always say "3 left", or sales that "end tonight" but the price is the same tomorrow.

Your customers see through this.

And once they catch you lying, they won't trust you again.

But when your urgency and scarcity is believable and can run continuously, it converts consistently without hurting your brand.

Here's what believable evergreen urgency and scarcity look like:

Seasonal offer rotation:

Keep the same core offer, rotate seasonal framing: "New Years Offer" → "Valentine's Special" → "Easter Promo" → "Summer Sale".

Have an active offer with a seasonal deadline that actually ends.

Production-based: 

"Limited batch production - once sold out, next batch ships in [X] weeks"

Inventory-based:

Display stock by size/flavor/variant based on the variant they picked - "Only 3 left in size M”

Ingredient/supply constraints:

"[Rare ingredient/material] restricts production to [X] units per month, only X left"

Social proof urgency:

"Bestseller - already sold out 4x this year"

Whatever tactics you use, they must be believable and you must honor them.

When you consistently honor them, customers learn to trust it. 

And trust is what drives action.
