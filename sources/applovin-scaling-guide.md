# The AppLovin Scaling Guide

The exact playbook we used to scale an e-commerce brand on AppLovin rewarded video — creatives, creative sets, end cards, campaigns, tracking, and aggressive scaling. Screenshots included.

Ricky — rare-brands.com · @ecomrickys on X · 2026 EDITION

## 01 — Why AppLovin, why right now

**GET $5K IN FREE AD SPEND.** AppLovin matches your first $5,000 in spend with another $5,000 on top — the cheapest testing budget you'll ever get on a new channel. To get the match, you need to sign up with this link: `ads.applovin.com/signup?referralCode=RAREBRANDS`. This guide is how you turn that budget into a scaling machine.

So what is AppLovin? It's ads inside mobile games. Think Candy Crush: someone runs out of lives, wants extra gems, wants to keep playing — and to get them, they have to watch a video. A full video, usually 30 seconds, sometimes 60. That video is where your brand goes.

Sit with that for a second, because it's the whole unlock. On Meta or TikTok you're fighting the thumb — you get half a second before they scroll. On AppLovin the viewer *chose* to watch your ad in exchange for a reward. You have their screen, uninterrupted, for 30 to 60 seconds. No feed, no competition, no scroll.

And the timing matters: AppLovin tends to favor new brands in their first couple of months on the platform. The algorithm gives newcomers a push. Combined with the spend match, the best moment to start was yesterday — the second best is today.

The system has three building blocks: the creatives (your videos), the creative sets (how you group them), and the campaigns (how you buy).

## 02 — How an AppLovin ad actually works

Every AppLovin ad your customer sees is a three-step experience. Video sells, end card converts.

- **Step 1 — Video.** 30–60s creative. Watched to completion — the viewer needs it to get their reward.
- **Step 2 — End card.** Full-screen "interactive": your offer, urgency, and a single Shop Now button.
- **Step 3 — Your store.** Tap-through lands on your product page, UTM-tagged so tracking sees everything.

Behind the scenes the structure mirrors Meta, with different names. You upload videos to the **Media library**. You group them into **creative sets** — one set per creative concept, the rough equivalent of an ad set. And creative sets get attached to **campaigns**, where you choose audience strategy, bid type, targets, and budget.

- **Block A — Creatives.** Your videos. By far the most important element in the whole system.
- **Block B — Creative sets.** One concept per set. Your videos + at least one end card. Start with five.
- **Block C — Campaigns.** Four at launch: Prospecting × Universal, each with tROAS and CPP versions.

## 03 — Block A: the videos do the heavy lifting

Everything else in this guide is setup. This is the part that decides whether you scale.

- **30s + 60s** — run both lengths. 60 seconds is the hard max.
- **Captions** — non-negotiable. Most viewers watch muted.
- **9:16** — vertical, full-screen, 1080×1920.

**Length.** The max is 60 seconds. Run two versions of your creatives: a 30-second cut and a 60-second cut, and test them against each other. You genuinely have the viewer's attention for the full duration — something no other channel gives you — so the long cut is worth testing, not just an afterthought.

**Captions.** Most people watch these videos with sound off — they're mid-game, often in public. If your video needs audio to make sense, it's dead on arrival. Use clean, professional captions — the standard styles straight out of TikTok or CapCut are exactly right. Nothing fancy.

**Caption placement rule:** divide the screen into four horizontal quarters. Your captions sit just above the bottom-quarter line — inside the upper three-quarters of the screen, never pinned to the very bottom. Ad UI and device chrome live in that bottom strip; captions parked there get covered or ignored.

One more time, because it's the thesis of this whole guide: end cards, campaign structure, bids — all of it matters far less than the video. Spend your energy here.

## 04 — Block B: one concept, one set

A creative set is AppLovin's grouping unit — the closest thing to an ad set on Facebook. The rule that makes them work: **one creative set corresponds to one creative concept.** Same idea, same angle, same style — multiple cuts and variations of it. What you never do is dump completely different creatives into one set. Mixed sets make performance unreadable.

**Start with five creative sets.** Five concepts is enough signal to find a winner without drowning your budget. You'll end up with a growing library of sets over time — we're in the hundreds of concepts now — but five is the right opening move.

**Name everything by number.** Every video and every set gets a numbered name — "Concept 296", "Creative Set 1225", whatever your system is. When you're filtering performance later and cutting losers, clean naming is the difference between a five-minute job and an archaeology dig.

**The upload flow.** Upload your videos to the Media library with your naming convention. Approval is fast — for us, around five minutes. Select all videos belonging to one concept, hit **Create creative set**, and name the set after the concept. Then you'll hit an error: *"You need at least one image in the creative set."* That's not a bug — that's AppLovin telling you the set needs an end card.

## 05 — Block B½: the end card — offer + urgency, nothing else

The end card — AppLovin calls it an **interactive**, some people say product card — is the clickable screen that appears when your video finishes. AppLovin even lets you custom-code fully playable interactives. You don't need any of that. Here's the recipe:

- **9:16 image** — one full-screen product visual as the background.
- **Urgency bar** — aggressive offer in the bottom third. "50% OFF — ENDS TONIGHT."
- **Shop Now** — one button. Delete everything else AppLovin auto-adds.

**Make it loud.** This card has one job: convert attention into a click. Ours run a hard offer with hard urgency — a big "50% OFF" with "ENDS TONIGHT" underneath, product front and center, Shop Now button at the bottom. Aggressive offer, aggressive urgency. Subtlety is for your brand book, not your end card.

**Strip the defaults.** When you build the card, AppLovin automatically adds extra elements — share buttons and other clutter. Delete all of it. Keep only the Shop Now button. One card, one action.

**Don't test end cards at the start.** Build your five end cards and use the **same five across all of your creative sets**. Different end cards per set makes early testing nearly impossible to read, and it isn't worth it anyway — as long as your offer and urgency are on the card, the end card is doing its job. The video is what you test. You can experiment with end cards later, once you're scaled.

*Setup note:* the end card is built in AppLovin's **Creative studio** (Media library → Interactives → Create → "Immersive visuals" template).

## 06 — Block C: the four-campaign launch matrix

AppLovin gives you a choice of audience strategy — **Prospecting** (optimize for customers who haven't bought from you) or **Universal** (optimize for purchases from everyone) — and a choice of goal type — **Target ROAS** or **Cost Per Purchase**. Don't pick. Launch all four combinations:

|  | Target ROAS | Cost Per Purchase |
|---|---|---|
| **Prospecting** | Campaign 1 — tROAS set ~10% above your real target | Campaign 2 — CPP set ~10% below your real target |
| **Universal** | Campaign 3 — tROAS set ~10% above your real target | Campaign 4 — CPP set ~10% below your real target |

**Why the aggressive targets?** Setting tROAS 10% higher (and CPP 10% lower) than what you actually need works like a cost cap on Facebook: if the platform can't deliver, it just won't spend, and you loosen until it does. For us, AppLovin has been good enough that it spent straight away even at the aggressive targets. Prospecting has been our best performer, but that varies by brand and attribution model — start with both and let the numbers decide.

**Budget: $200/day per campaign.** Attach **all five creative sets to all four campaigns**, then manage it like Meta — watch which sets perform, cut what doesn't, feed what does.

**Two settings that matter.** *Dynamic Catalog: OFF.* AppLovin will push you hard to enable it, quoting a 25% lift. Leave it off — you want your designed creatives and end cards running, not auto-generated catalog ads. · *Naming:* name campaigns by their settings, e.g. "Prospecting – tROAS 2.5 – USA – Mug", so the dashboard reads itself.

## 07 — Tracking: one attribution model to rule every platform

AppLovin's in-platform reporting gives you options: Day 0 ROAS, Day 7 ROAS, different revenue definitions. When we started, we deliberately ran the harshest one — **D0 checkout revenue** — because we wanted to punish the platform as much as possible and see whether it was genuinely incremental. It was. Sales showed up that no other channel could claim same-day.

Looking back, you can skip the paranoia phase: **connect your tracker (we use Triple Whale) from day one and use the exact same attribution model you already use to scale Facebook.** For us that's **linear paid, 7 days**. Whatever yours is — the non-negotiable part is that it's identical across every platform you buy on.

> As AppLovin's spend grew, Facebook started claiming AppLovin's sales as its own. One unified attribution model across platforms is not optional — it's the only way to see who's actually driving revenue.

That pull quote is the expensive lesson. Early on, in-platform numbers were clean enough that we scaled AppLovin off its own reporting — and used the incremental lift to justify Facebook too. But the bigger AppLovin got, the more of its sales Meta's attribution quietly absorbed. Every platform grades its own homework. The referee has to be a third party, on one model, everywhere.

*Setup notes.* Spend reports back into Triple Whale on the same basis as Facebook (7-day click). In the campaign's URL builder, set your UTMs (`utm_source=applovin`, `utm_medium=paid`, campaign + creative set macros) and add **Triple Whale as a measurement partner** — it auto-appends the `tw_` parameters. · Match your campaign **Day target** to your attribution window: we run **Day 7**, consistent with linear paid 7 days.

## 08 — Scaling: when it works, hit it hard

Here's where AppLovin behaves differently from every other platform you've scaled. On Meta, doubling a budget overnight usually torches performance and sends the campaign back into learning. AppLovin is remarkably reactive to aggressive scaling. We have doubled and even quadrupled budgets in the same day without breaking delivery.

- **2–4×** — same-day budget increases we've run without breaking performance.
- **~2 months** — the honeymoon window where AppLovin favors new brands.
- **Day 1** — when to start scaling: as soon as you see results + incrementality.

Remember the honeymoon period: AppLovin tends to give new brands a push during their first couple of months. That window is a use-it-or-lose-it asset. The worst thing you can do is find a working setup and drip-feed it $50 increments while the algorithm is actively trying to help you.

So the playbook is simple. Launch the four-campaign matrix. Watch your unified attribution — not the platform's self-reported numbers. The moment you see real results and real incrementality, **scale hard and scale fast**. Double budgets. Double again. Keep feeding the winners fresh creative concepts as new sets, keep the same five end cards, and let the aggressive targets protect your downside — if performance slips, the campaigns simply stop spending, and you adjust.

## 09 — The launch checklist

**Creatives**
- 30s and 60s cuts of each concept, 9:16, 60s max — test both lengths
- Clean TikTok/CapCut-style captions — placed just above the bottom-quarter line, never pinned to the bottom

**Creative sets & end cards**
- 5 creative sets — one concept per set, never mixed; number everything (videos and sets)
- 5 end cards — 9:16 image, aggressive offer + urgency, Shop Now only (delete auto-added extras)
- Same 5 end cards in every set — don't test end cards until you're scaled

**Campaigns**
- 4 campaigns: Prospecting + Universal × tROAS + CPP — tROAS ~10% above target, CPP ~10% below
- $200/day per campaign — all creative sets in all campaigns; Dynamic Catalog OFF
- Day target = your attribution window (ours: Day 7) — name campaigns by their settings

**Tracking & scaling**
- UTMs + Triple Whale (or your tracker) set in the campaign URL builder from day one
- One attribution model across all platforms (ours: linear paid, 7 days) — never trust each platform's self-reporting as spend grows
- Scale aggressively the moment you see results + incrementality — 2–4× same-day is fine; use the new-brand honeymoon
- Cut and feed weekly — kill losing sets, ship new concepts as new sets, Meta-style

## Appendix A — Setting up Creative Sets, step by step

Nine steps from empty Media library to a live creative set with videos and an end card.

- **A1 Upload your videos.** Go to **Media** → **Upload** (top right). Upload every cut of your concepts, named by your numbering convention. Approval usually clears in about five minutes.
- **A2 Select one concept's videos → Create creative set.** Once approved, tick every video belonging to **one** concept — note the clean numbered names and the 1080×1920 dimensions — then click **Create creative set**.
- **A3 Name the set after the concept.** Name the set by its concept number (here: "Concept 296"). The Assets panel shows 8 videos, 0 interactives. That zero is what we fix next; without an image or interactive the set can't go live. Use **+ Add more** to manage assets.
- **A4 Interactives tab → Create.** In the Media Library pop-up, switch to the **Interactives** tab and hit **Create**. This opens AppLovin's Creative studio where you build the end card. (Ours show "10 creative sets", "13 creative sets" — same cards everywhere.)
- **A5 Pick the "Immersive visuals" template.** AppLovin offers Carousel (3–9 images/videos), Feature highlights (1 image/video), Quiz (1 link / up to 5 questions). Skip the clever stuff. **Immersive visuals** (1 image / video) is the one: full-screen visual, CTA button, done.
- **A6 Swap in your own 9:16 image.** In Creative studio, name the asset ("End card 1") and click **Select asset** to replace the sample placeholder with your own 9:16 visual (JPG/PNG, 900×1600 works). The left sidebar (Asset, CTA, Logo, Overlay, Text) is where AppLovin auto-adds extras — delete everything except the Shop Now button.
- **A7 Offer + urgency + Shop Now → Save asset.** The finished recipe live: big **50% OFF**, **ENDS TONIGHT**, product hero shot, single Shop Now button. Hit **Save asset**.
- **A8 Back in the set → Add more → pick your interactive.** Your new interactive now appears in the library. The preview panel shows the full ad flow being assembled: Step 1 Video → Step 2 Interactive → Step 3 Endcard.
- **A9 Select it, Confirm — then Save as live.** Tick your interactive (approved, $0 spend so far) and hit **Confirm**. Back on the set page, hit **Save as live**. Repeat until you have five creative sets — same five end cards in each.

## Appendix B — Setting up Campaigns, step by step

Run through this flow four times — Prospecting and Universal, each in a tROAS and a CPP version.

- **B1 Campaigns → Create.** From the Campaigns dashboard, hit **Create**. Note the attribution reporting toggle up top (Clicks / Cohort view) — this dashboard is where you later filter winners and losers, Meta-style.
- **B2 Objective — name it by its settings, Dynamic Catalog OFF.** Campaign type: **Web conversion**. Name the campaign after its configuration — "Prospecting – Troas 2.5 – USA – Mug". Then leave **"Enable Dynamic Catalog" OFF**. AppLovin warns about a claimed 25% lift — ignore it.
- **B3 Campaign URL — UTMs + Triple Whale.** In the URL Builder: destination URL is your product page, then `utm_source=applovin`, `utm_medium=paid`, and the built-in macros for campaign name (`{CAMPAIGN_NAME}`) and creative set (`{CREATIVE_SET}`). If you're on Triple Whale, add it as a **measurement partner** — it auto-appends the `tw_` tracking parameters. Confirm.
- **B4 Targeting — specific countries.** Choose **Specific countries/regions** and select your market — here, United States, all states. One geo per campaign keeps the naming clean and the results readable.
- **B5 Optimization — goal type × audience strategy.** Goal type: ROAS or CPP (CPE also exists). Audience strategy: Universal / Prospecting / Discovery. This build is Prospecting + ROAS — campaign 1 of 4. Repeat for the other three combinations.
- **B6 Day target + goal — Day 7, tROAS ~10% above target.** Day target: **Day 7**, matching linear-paid-7-days attribution (use Day 0 only if your product converts same-session). Goal entered as a percentage — 250% = 2.5× ROAS. Set roughly 10% above what you actually need; on CPP campaigns set cost ~10% below. If it doesn't spend, loosen.
- **B7 Budget — $200/day, start immediately.** Combined daily budget across all countries/regions: $200 per campaign (valid range $10–$10,000,000). Daily spend may vary and occasionally exceed it — that's normal. Leave the end date off; start date is tomorrow (dates run on UTC).
- **B8 Creative Sets → Add new.** Scroll to the Creative Sets block and hit **+ Add new** to attach your sets. All five creative sets go into all four campaigns.
- **B9 Select your live sets → Confirm.** Tick your live creative sets in the picker and hit **Confirm**. (Their library shows 80 sets; yours starts with five.)
- **B10 Create campaign — then build the other three.** Hit **Create campaign**. Run the same flow three more times — Prospecting/CPP, Universal/tROAS, Universal/CPP — and the launch setup is complete: 4 campaigns × 5 creative sets × $200/day.
