Had a customer last month make an order of £500+ on our UK store.

Delivered to their address, signed for, everything looked normal.

Two weeks later, chargeback hits my account.

Reason: "Product not received"

Bank sided with the customer.

Lost the product, lost the money, got hit with a chargeback fee on top.

Customer kept everything and got a full refund.

But here's the real problem: this drives up your chargeback rate (even if you haven't done anything wrong)

Most payment processors will hold your funds or even terminate your account if your chargeback rate hits 1%.

With Q4 coming up, that's the last thing you want.

It's literally digital shoplifting.

So how to prevent this?

After dealing with this BS multiple times, I looked into fraud prevention tools.

Now I use @chargeflow. They track buyer behavior patterns across thousands of merchants.

If someone has a history of filing bogus chargebacks, you know before they even complete checkout.

So you keep your goods, save time & keep your sanity (instead of dealing with endless documentation).

This problem compounds during Q4 when transaction volumes spike.

So don't risk a record Q4 to fraudulent behavior and chargebacks.
