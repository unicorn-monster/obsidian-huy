Blackout Water is ripping with an advertorial on "Your Family Has 72 Hours. Here's What Happens After That."

Their top ad has been running 120 days...

It frames clean water as a grid issue, not plumbing.

When the grid drops, the pumps stop & the tap runs dry.

The click lands on an editorial site called THE OUTSIDER.

An author byline, & a dark kitchen lit by a lantern.

The subhead calls out a "$25 fix" before storm season ends.

I like how the copy hooks the reader's prepper identity:

"You've thought about this more than most people."

It validates the food in the pantry & the half-assembled go-bag.

Then it pulls the rug:

"Your municipal water supply doesn't run on gravity."

Mid-scroll, a pop-up cuts right over the story.

A photo shows dirty water next to a clean glass for a kid.

The decline link is brutal:

"No thanks, I'll pay full price" {the classic shameful no}.

On a cheap survival offer, gear specs do not PRINT.

Nobody wakes up shopping for a plastic straw.

If you have food but no water, you're cooked.

At the bottom, the button doesn't say buy.

It says "PREPARE TODAY!"

By the offer, paying $25 doesn't feel like buying camping gear.

It feels like finishing the prep they already started.
