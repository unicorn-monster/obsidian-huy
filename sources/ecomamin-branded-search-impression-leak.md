meaning HUNDREDS of high-intent branded searches every month bleed to competitor ads, organic clutter, and the map pack instead of landing on the brand's own listing

and the team had no idea. let me show you how this happens

the brand spends $40k a month on meta. the creative works. buyers get curious, close instagram, open google 20 minutes later, and type in the brand name

sitting right there at the top of the results is a competitor running a "[brand name] alternative" ad

the competitor spends $3k a month to intercept $40k worth of buyer intent someone else created

$3k to steal $40k. the arbitrage is running against your brand right now

and branded search is only one of the five leaks

your shopping titles lead with your brand name instead of the keyword the buyer typed. google indexes the first three words the hardest, and mobile shows four max. your product goes invisible to category searches your meta ads generated

we split-tested keyword-first vs brand-first titles on one account. conversion rate doubled from rearranging words already in the title

your competitors are building "[your brand] vs [their brand]" pages and intercepting your highest-intent buyers at the exact moment they're about to purchase

your competitors run google display, youtube, and gmail remarketing, getting 5-10 touchpoints on the buyer while you run one meta retargeting ad on one platform

and bing? 6-8% of search market share, higher-income users, CPCs 30-50% lower than google, competition nearly zero. we launched a fresh bing account and pulled $20k from $2.8k in four weeks at 7x ROAS

every dollar of meta spend generating awareness for a buyer who later searches on google is a dollar funding whoever owns the google real estate for those queries

your meta ads aren't underperforming because of creative fatigue

they're underperforming because you're creating demand on meta and letting competitors capture the sale on google

check your auction insights, look at impression share on your brand name, and search "[your brand] reviews" to see who owns the SERP
