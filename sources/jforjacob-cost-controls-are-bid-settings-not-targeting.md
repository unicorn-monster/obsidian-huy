title: "Cost caps and bid caps are bid settings, not optimisation settings"
---

One of the dumbest things I see on Twitter is the thought that using cost controls means you're optimising for people mid to lower in the funnel or only people who are ready to "buy now"

1. I think the people that say this automatically think that cost controls have no flexibility. When using target CPA for example you will rarely have a day when you come in on target. Some days will be much higher, some days will be much lower but It will average out over your chosen attribution window. In the days it's coming in above, what do you think is happening?

2. Setting a cost control doesn't mean you're setting it so low that you can only capture warm audiences. Let's say your break even CPA was $50 and your account average was $30. What do think would happen if you set up a cost controlled campaign with a target CPA of $100? You think you're only going after bottom of funnel? Please

3. People act like you can't change them. Got plenty of cash in the bank and are happy to spend more at less efficiency? Up your caps. A little tight on cash and need ensure more efficiency? Lower your caps. You can choose how efficient you want to be or how much you're willing to spend on 'driving more awareness' at least this way you have some kind of control over it

4. Bid settings like cost caps and bid caps are exactly that, bid settings not optimisation settings. The setting you can change that will make sure you're going after people ready to buy now is by changing your attribution setting to 1 day click, that will effect your optimisation and targeting toward the lower end of the funnel, not your bid type
