Cialdini's Influence is probably the most useful book I've ever read for marketing.

I re-read it this week and realized almost every email I've written that actually converted was leaning on one of his 7 principles.

So here's how to use each one in your email strategy so you can plug them into your next campaign:

1) Scarcity: People want things more when they're harder to get, and they move faster when they think someone else might get there first. Instead of just running a deadline like "ends 11/28," limit it to the first 100 people and put a midnight cutoff on top of that. Name the code FIRST100 so they're reminded of the cap at checkout.

2) Authority: People trust credentials more than they trust claims. Instead of your brand explaining why the product works, have a doctor or an expert say it in their own words, and lead with who they are. Bring in the product and the CTA after they've made the case, not before.

3) Unity: People say yes to brands they see as one of their own. Instead of generic reviews, show real customers using the product with their handles and their own words next to them. Write the copy like you're talking to someone who's already part of the group instead of someone you're trying to sell to.

4) Commitment: Once someone takes a small action with you, they're more likely to take a bigger one. Instead of leading with the offer, lead with something they have to click to see, like a free mystery box or a hidden discount. Get them to say yes to something small first.

5) Liking: People buy from people they like, and that doesn't change over email. Instead of another branded send, write it from the founder in first person with a real photo of them. Open with something your reader is already feeling, give three or four useful tips, and put the product at the very end.

6) Reciprocity: When someone helps you first, you feel like you owe them one. Instead of sending a discount, send a how-to with real steps and numbers that's useful whether they buy or not. Put the offer at the very bottom, after you've already given them something.

7) Social proof: People copy what others like them do, but only when they believe it's real. Instead of a star rating or three short quotes, build the whole email around one customer, use their name, and quote exactly what they said. Save your review count for the bottom as backup.
