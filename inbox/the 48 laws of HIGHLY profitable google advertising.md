robert greene wrote about power. these are the rules for printing money on google.

some of them are clean. some of them sit in the grey area between competitive and ruthless. all of them work. and the brands who follow them consistently are the ones scaling past seven figures on google while their competitors wonder why more budget isn't fixing the problem.

i've spent seven years building these rules across [$10M+](https://x.com/search?q=%2410M%2B&src=cashtag_click) in managed ad spend. every law is grounded in something i've seen work in a live account, not in theory.

**law 1: never let your brand name go undefended.**

your competitors are bidding on your brand name right now. every branded search click they intercept is a customer you paid to create on meta, youtube, or organic, handed to them for the cost of a CPC. run branded search protection before anything else touches the account.

**law 2: bid on your competitors' brand names without shame.**

conquest campaigns are the mirror image of law 1. the buyer who searches a competitor's name has demonstrated purchase intent in the category. a comparison page that redirects that intent to your product converts at 3-4%. this is not unethical. it's competitive. every brand in the market is eligible to bid on every keyword. the ones who do it win.

**law 3: the product page is for closers, not for strangers.**

cold traffic sent to a product page bounces. warm traffic sent to a product page converts. the PDP's job is to close the sale after something else has done the educating. never use the PDP as a cold traffic destination.

notice how this Grün's product page is heavily optimized with checkout options, subscriptions, and high-intent conversion levers. sending cold traffic here fails because it treats strangers like buyers who are already sold on a product.

**law 4: the shopping title is a keyword, not a label.**

the first 60 characters of your shopping title determine which searches trigger the listing. most brands waste those characters on their brand name, which cold searchers don't know. put the converting non-branded keywords first. brand name goes at the end.

**law 5: free shipping is a conversion weapon, not a perk.**

two identical listings. one shows a shipping cost. one shows "free shipping." the free shipping listing wins the click at a rate that makes every brand not using it look negligent. price the shipping into the product cost and set merchant center shipping to $0.

**law 6: a lifestyle image beats a product shot in the shopping grid.**

if every competitor uses white background product shots, your lifestyle image stands out. the listing that looks different in a sea of sameness gets the click. screenshot your highest-volume shopping queries. do the opposite of whatever the majority is doing visually.

**law 7: the ROAS number lies unless you segment it.**

blended account ROAS includes branded search, which inflates the number because branded converts at 5-10x cold traffic rates. pull branded separately. if 80% of your "7x ROAS" comes from branded, your cold traffic ROAS is closer to 1.5x. know the real number before making decisions.

**law 8: PMax is a cleanup layer, not a strategy.**

one PMax campaign with all products and no signals is not a strategy. it's a delegation of every decision to an algorithm with no useful information. PMax belongs at the bottom of the funnel, capturing what dedicated campaigns miss. it should never be the primary mechanism.

**law 9: never switch bid strategies before you have the data.**

target ROAS requires 30-50 conversions per campaign per month to optimise. switching to target ROAS on day one with 3 conversions is asking the algorithm to guess. launch on maximise clicks or maximise conversions without a target. let the data accumulate. the trigger is the conversion count, not the calendar.

**law 10: your meta ads are google search campaigns in disguise.**

every meta impression generates a google search 3-7 days later. the buyer who sees your instagram ad and doesn't click will search your brand name, your product category, or your problem keyword on google when they're ready. if your google setup catches that search, you close the sale you already paid meta to create. if it doesn't, a competitor does.

**law 11: negative keywords are more valuable than positive ones.**

an account running for 12 months with no negative keyword hygiene is donating 15-30% of its budget to irrelevant queries. pull the search terms report monthly. add every non-converting term to a master negative list. the budget you save goes further than the budget you spend.

**law 12: the advertorial is the most powerful weapon for cold traffic.**

a 1,200-word editorial page between the ad and the PDP doubles cold traffic CVR in almost every niche. the advertorial educates the buyer, builds trust, and positions the product as the logical answer to the question they searched. the PDP then closes a pre-sold prospect instead of a sceptical stranger.

**law 13: never host your advertorial on your main domain.**

the moment the reader sees the brand's domain, logo, and navigation on the advertorial, they process it as advertising. host it on a separate domain or subdomain. different design. different header. the reader should think they found an article, not that they landed on a branded page.

**law 14: every campaign needs its own landing page.**

the same landing page serving branded search, non-branded search, shopping, and demand gen is a guarantee of mediocre conversion across all of them. match the page to the traffic temperature. match the message to the search intent.

**law 15: build the audience before you build the campaign.**

upload customer match lists. build custom intent audiences from converting search terms. layer in-market segments. feed YouTube view audiences as signals. the algorithm performs categorically differently when it has real buyer data to start from versus guessing from scratch.

**law 16: youtube shorts is the most underpriced upper funnel placement in google.**

CPMs on youtube shorts for ecom categories are significantly lower than equivalent meta placements. a well-structured shorts campaign builds a 50,000-person view audience in 60-90 days at a budget most brands spend on a single week of meta retargeting.

**law 17: repurpose your winning meta creatives for youtube instead of starting from zero.**

validated meta video ads already have proven hooks, pacing, and angles. reformat them for 9:16 vertical, strip the meta-specific CTAs, and run them on youtube shorts through demand gen. the creative testing phase drops from weeks to days because the angle is already validated.

**law 18: test 10 creatives per week, kill 8 of them.**

most brands test 2-3 creatives per month and wonder why nothing scales. the winning creative is hidden inside a volume of tests. run 10 variants, give them 7 days at $30/day each, kill everything with a view-through rate below 40% or a CTR below 1.5%, and double the budget on the survivors.

**law 19: the hook decides everything in the first 3 seconds.**

on youtube shorts, the viewer is actively consuming content. the opt-out is a single swipe. if the first 3 seconds don't stop them, the remaining 57 seconds don't exist. front-load the most visually arresting, emotionally triggering, or curiosity-provoking moment in the creative. the hook is the ad.

**law 20: google's algorithm rewards consistency over intensity.**

accounts that run steady budgets for 90+ days outperform accounts that spike and crash. the algorithm learns from consistent data. budget swings of 50%+ in a single day reset the learning phase and destabilise performance for 7-14 days. scale in 20-30% increments, never 2x overnight.

**law 21: the comparison page wins by being honest.**

comparison pages that show the brand winning on every metric are propaganda. the buyer recognises propaganda immediately and leaves. admit where a competitor wins on price. then explain why the factors the brand wins on matter more for the buyer's specific need. honesty converts. advocacy doesn't.

**law 22: conquest campaigns fund themselves within 30 days.**

the CPA on conquest campaigns (bidding on competitor brand names) is higher than branded search but lower than non-branded search, because the buyer has demonstrated category purchase intent. the revenue from intercepting even 5% of a competitor's branded traffic typically covers the entire conquest budget within the first month.

**law 23: the quiz funnel is a commitment machine, not a lead form.**

every question the buyer answers increases their psychological investment in the outcome. by question 5, they've spent 60-90 seconds telling you about their needs. walking away from the recommendation means admitting they wasted that time. the quiz doesn't sell the product. the quiz sells the commitment to hearing the recommendation.

**law 24: throw confetti between quiz questions.**

micro-celebrations (confetti animations, checkmark flashes, "great choice!" messages) between quiz questions increase completion rates by 15-25%. the dopamine hit reinforces the commitment loop and makes the quiz feel rewarding rather than tedious. this sounds gimmicky. the numbers say otherwise.

**law 25: star ratings on shopping listings close the sale before the click.**

a listing showing 4.7 stars from 2,400 reviews converts clicks at a fundamentally different rate than the same listing with no rating. the minimum threshold for display is 50 reviews at 3.5 stars or above. review volume is a business priority, not a vanity metric. it directly affects shopping revenue.

**law 26: never run a youtube ad that looks like a television commercial.**

polished, high-production brand videos underperform raw, UGC-style content on youtube shorts because the native content on the platform is raw. the viewer's pattern recognition flags polished content as advertising before the hook lands. match the platform's native aesthetic. direct-to-camera, unscripted feel, real environments, phone-quality production.

**law 27: segment PMax by ICP, not by product category.**

one PMax campaign per buyer persona, not one PMax campaign for all products. each asset group gets its own creative set, headline strategy, and landing page destination. the buyer who needs joint support supplements at age 55 responds to completely different creative than the athlete who needs recovery supplements at 28. serve them different creative. send them to different pages.

**law 28: the search terms report is an intelligence document, not a maintenance task.**

the search terms report tells you exactly what queries your budget is going to. the brands that read it monthly find new title keywords, new negative keywords, new product angles, and new campaign ideas they'd never have discovered otherwise. the brands that ignore it are flying blind.

**law 29: broad match without negatives is a budget donation to google.**

broad match keyword "supplement" will show your ad for "supplement store near me", "supplement meaning in hindi", and "supplement my income working from home." every irrelevant impression is a wasted click waiting to happen. build the negative keyword architecture before going broad. or stay on phrase match.

**law 30: the remarketing pool is the most valuable asset in the account.**

every touchpoint generates an audience. youtube viewers, advertorial readers, quiz completers, PDP visitors, cart abandoners. each one is warmer than the next. the brands that build and segment these audiences and feed them back into the campaign structure as signals are compounding. the brands that don't are paying cold-traffic CPAs on traffic that should be warm.

**law 31: your feed title strategy should change with the seasons.**

seasonal keywords ("christmas gift for dad", "summer running shoes") should rotate into your shopping feed titles during peak periods and rotate out afterward. the brands doing this capture seasonal search volume that generic titles miss. the brands not doing it lose to competitors who do.

**law 32: urgency in google ads is structural, not cosmetic.**

"limited time offer" and countdown timers in ad copy are cosmetic urgency. structural urgency is a promotional price fed through merchant center that displays a "was/now" comparison and a "special offer" badge directly in the shopping listing. the second type moves CPCs. the first type gets ignored.

**law 33: the email sequence is the funnel's recovery mechanism.**

70-90% of buyers who engage with the funnel don't purchase on the first visit. the 5-email post-opt-in sequence recovers 15-25% of them. email 1 delivers. email 2 educates. email 3 proves. email 4 handles objections. email 5 closes. most brands running a generic "thanks for downloading" email followed by a weekly newsletter recover 3-5%.

**law 34: the google ads transparency center is free competitive intelligence.**

search any competitor's brand name on adstransparency(.)google(.)com and see every ad they're running across every format. click through to see their landing pages. screenshot the structure. this costs nothing and takes 10 minutes per competitor. the brands who do this monthly have a permanent information advantage.

**law 35: micro-conversions should be your primary conversion event in the first 30 days.**

before enough purchase events exist to train smart bidding, set the primary conversion to add-to-cart or initiate checkout. this gives the algorithm 3-5x more signal to learn from in the learning phase. switch to purchase as the primary conversion once the campaign is generating 30-50 purchases per month.

**law 36: display ads and demand gen are awareness tools, not conversion tools.**

measure demand gen and display on remarketing pool growth, branded search volume trend, and MOF/BOF CPA improvement. not on last-click ROAS. evaluating awareness campaigns on conversion metrics is like evaluating seeds by whether they taste good today.

**law 37: the landing page loads in under 3 seconds or the click is wasted.**

google's own data: conversion rates drop 20% for every additional second of load time above 3 seconds. a $50 CPC click on a page that takes 6 seconds to load is a $50 donation to google with a 40% performance penalty built in. audit page speed before auditing campaigns.

**law 38: custom labels in the shopping feed are the segmentation tool nobody uses.**

label products by margin tier, performance tier, seasonality, and promotional status. use those labels to build separate shopping campaigns: high-margin heroes with aggressive bids, new products in a testing phase, clearance items with promotional pricing. the algorithm should execute your profit logic, not its own.

**law 39: the buyer on bing is not the buyer on google.**

bing skews older, higher income, less exposed to blackhat ecom advertising, and less price-sensitive. CPAs on bing for supplement, health, furniture, and home categories run 15-25% lower than equivalent google campaigns. bing is not a smaller google. it's a parallel demand pool with a different buyer profile.

**law 40: the founder ad outperforms the brand ad on youtube.**

a 60-second founder-to-camera video explaining why the product exists converts cold traffic at higher rates than a polished brand video because the scepticism reflex is trained to distrust brands, not people. a recognisable human behind the product reads as accountable in a way that a logo never does.

**law 41: your shopping image should sell the result, not the product.**

a product image shows what the buyer gets. a lifestyle image shows who the buyer becomes. for categories where the emotional outcome is the purchase driver (fitness, sleep, skincare, wellness), the lifestyle image outperforms the product shot in the shopping grid because it sells the transformation.

**law 42: A/B test one variable at a time or learn nothing.**

testing a new headline, new image, and new landing page simultaneously tells you nothing about what caused the performance change. one variable per test. one hypothesis per test. documented control. documented measurement criteria. the brands that compound performance over time are the ones with a testing discipline, not the ones with bigger budgets.

**law 43: the "ships from UK" label is a trust signal.**

in an era where buyers have been burned by products shipping from Shenzhen with 6-week delivery times, "ships from UK" (or "ships from US") in the shopping listing implies domestic speed and legitimate operation. set your merchant center shipping settings to reflect your actual shipping origin clearly.

**law 44: YouTube long-form ads build the deepest trust at the lowest CPM.**

a 5-8 minute youtube video that walks through the product mechanism, addresses the main objections, and presents the founder's story converts non-buying search visitors at meaningful rates. the trust barrier that kills a 30-second social ad doesn't apply when the buyer has watched an 8-minute product demonstration.

**law 45: never repeat the same proof point across two funnel pages.**

the buyer who reads the advertorial, then the comparison page, then the PDP should see three different proof layers. not the same testimonial three times. distribute proof across the funnel. each touchpoint adds new credibility. repetition signals laziness, not authority.

**law 46: the winning creative is always the one you didn't expect.**

the creative you spent the most time on will not outperform the one you threw together in 10 minutes. this happens with enough consistency that it should change your production process. generate volume. test fast. let the data pick the winner. creative intuition is useful for generating variants. it is unreliable for predicting winners.

**law 47: the account structure should be decided before the first dollar is spent.**

the keyword architecture, the campaign segmentation, the funnel type per traffic tier, the audience signal library, the negative keyword categories, the bid strategy sequence: all of this should exist as a document before any campaign goes live. the brands that build the structure first and then spend inside it outperform the brands that start spending and retrofit the structure around whatever is happening.

**law 48: the ecosystem compounds. individual campaigns don't.**

a single campaign produces linear results. an ecosystem, youtube building the remarketing pool that feeds search that feeds shopping that feeds branded that feeds email, produces compounding results. the efficiency improves over time without proportional increases in spend. the ceiling rises with every month of accumulated audience data. the brands at [$100k+](https://x.com/search?q=%24100k%2B&src=cashtag_click) per month on google are not the brands with the biggest budgets. they're the brands that understood the ecosystem before their competitors did.
